# Working plan assumptions and calculations for architect/engineer review

**Update 2026-09-30:** The numerical examples below are a historical snapshot of the first review geometry. The current Plan 1/5 geometry, measurements, remaining defects and calculations are in [TEN-PLAN-ARCHITECT-REVIEW.md](TEN-PLAN-ARCHITECT-REVIEW.md). In particular, the current stair is 2,600 × 4,585 mm with 255 mm treads, the under-stair tank is 2,100 × 3,900 × 977 mm net wet dimensions, Plan 1 is a 9.25 × 8.85 m compact envelope, and its frame reserves 12 columns. Do not apply the older 5,200 mm stair, 270 mm tread, 4,200 mm tank length or old axis lists below to current drawings.

Date: 2026-09-30. This document describes the **local design model** used by `local/runtime/nepal-plan-review/index.html` and the Studio's **Open working plan review** button. The owner is a Nepal Engineering Council-registered architect/engineer and can review the drawings directly. Review feedback can change the model without waiting for a separate reviewer. These inputs are accepted for design exploration, not asserted as parcel-specific adopted permit rules.

## Reproducible calculations: 11.25 m × 11.25 m reference plot

Both supplied rectangle fixtures use this plot. It is **126.5625 m²**, approximately 3.98 aana under the conversion in the supplied building resource book (4 aana = 1,369 ft² = 127.1843 m²). The four 1 m working setbacks leave **9.25 × 9.25 = 85.5625 m²** of buildable rectangle. The current rectangle hypothesis occupies it. Working coverage is **85.5625 / 126.5625 = 0.676049**, or **67.60%**. A 70% working cap would permit **88.59375 m²**, so this rectangle is **3.03125 m² below that working cap**. These numbers are geometric; no legal FAR/coverage determination is implied.

The 2.5-storey fixture has two full 85.5625 m² envelopes and an integer-mm-generated 50.0053 m² partial envelope: **2 × 85.5625 + 50.0053 = 221.1303 m²** summed plan envelopes. The partial-floor target was 50 m²; the extra 0.0053 m² is the current geometry quantization difference, not bonus permitted area. The 3.5-storey rental fixture has three full envelopes and a 40 m² partial envelope: **3 × 85.5625 + 40 = 296.6875 m²**. Dividing the latter by plot area gives **2.3442**, an *illustrative envelope/site ratio*, **not FAR**. Stair slab voids, regulatory exclusions and wall construction are not subtracted.

For a 3,000 mm floor-to-floor rise at the provisional 190 mm maximum riser, `ceil(3000/190) = 16` risers. Two equal flights have **8 risers each**, actual rise **3000/16 = 187.5 mm**. Each flight has 7 plan treads at 270 mm, so run is **7 × 270 = 1,890 mm**. Required working core width is `2 × 1,000 + 200 centre allowance + 400 edge allowances = 2,600 mm`. Required plan length is `1,400 arrival pad + 1,890 flight run + 1,000 intermediate landing + 400 edge allowances = 4,690 mm`; the reserved core is **2,600 × 5,200 mm**. The 1,000 mm unit entry lies within the 1,400 mm *floor-level* pad. The half-storey landing is not used as a unit entrance. The drawing does not establish 2,000 mm beam/soffit headroom.

The reservoir internal plan area is **2.1 × 4.2 = 8.82 m²**. To reach 8,000 L, required wet depth is `ceil(8,000 L ÷ 8.82 m²) = 908 mm` (after unit conversion). Resulting net volume is **2.1 × 4.2 × 0.908 = 8.00856 m³ = 8,008.56 L**. A further 200 mm freeboard is provisionally recorded; structural thickness and excavation depth are not.

For example, a room with 15.0 m² gross area has a provisional hilly-region light-opening demand of **15.0 / 10 = 1.5 m²**. With an assumed 1.2 m *clear* opening height, the minimum reserved width is `ceil(1.5 / 1.2 × 1000) = 1,250 mm`. The algorithm currently sizes apertures from gross room boxes, while clear room labels deduct nominal wall thickness; this is conservative for the opening ratio but does not establish daylight quality, legal exposure, frame dimensions or an approved boundary window.

## New structural-bay preference and the reference plot

The owner set a **preferred 10-14 ft planning span** and wants bedrooms and kitchens to occupy one four-column grid cell where possible. Conversion: `10 x 304.8 = 3,048 mm`; `14 x 304.8 = 4,267.2 mm`. Integer-mm geometry caps each adjacent planning-axis interval at **4,267 mm**. The grid now inserts an intermediate axis if the old corridor-anchored interval exceeds that cap. These are provisional grid axes, **not verified beam spans**: actual column continuity at L/court voids, beam paths, member sizing, seismic response and foundations still require engineering.

For the 11.25 m square reference plot, 1 m setbacks produce a **9.25 m** wide footprint. The west side-core layout uses a **2.60 m stair core + 1.102 m corridor wall-line width**, leaving **5.548 m** for the main room strip. A bedroom or kitchen spanning that whole strip is **5.548 / 0.3048 = 18.20 ft** wide and crosses a proposed intermediate grid axis. Splitting the strip into two equal room widths would yield **2.774 m = 9.10 ft**, below the preferred 10 ft lower end. Two 10 ft cells plus core and corridor require **6.096 + 2.60 + 1.102 = 9.798 m**, **548 mm more** than this footprint provides. The current strip plan therefore does **not** meet the four-column room-cell preference; it reports the affected rooms as `MAIN_ROOM_GRID_CELL_NOT_MET`. A different core/circulation/room arrangement or a changed verified site envelope is required, rather than hiding the conflict.

On the first selected owner reference option, x axes are **1,175 / 3,425 / 4,877 / 7,476 / 10,075 mm** (intervals **2.250 / 1.452 / 2.599 / 2.599 m**) and y axes are **1,175 / 4,309 / 6,940 / 10,075 mm** (intervals **3.134 / 2.631 / 3.135 m**). The maximum 3.135 m interval is below 14 ft, but several intervals are shorter than the preferred 10 ft. The 350 mm planning columns are drawn; room labels deduct nominal 102/229 mm brick walls, but not plaster or columns protruding into a clear room. The 10 ft lower end is a goal, not a rule that causes otherwise honest small-plot concepts to disappear. The owner's phrase “14 ft × 14 ft beams” is treated as an approximately 14 ft bay in both plan directions, **not** as a beam cross-section. Beam depth and width require structural design.

## What to mark up in the review sheet

The plan viewer shows three current alternatives per fixture, every level, clear room area/dimensions after nominal brick bands, stair risers, column reservations, door/window intervals, and rejected search attempts. It is intentionally detailed enough to expose errors. The largest known design problems are: all selected options still share a full rectangular footprint; the 1 m nominal clear corridor does not deduct plaster/putty/paint; some columns cross large main rooms despite the four-column-room preference; attached baths, balcony projections, parking, furniture, kitchen counters and wet shafts are unplaced; and the upper floor slab/stair opening, roof, elevations and building section are not drawn. A planner-suggested room is marked with an asterisk and must be accepted or changed by the owner. An entry not aligned opposite a living door is reported, not claimed to meet the usual living-entry convention.

The owner can mark corrections by **fixture, option, floor and room ID**. Example: `rental-3_5 / rectangle-west-living-first / second / second-puja — reduce depth and protect a living-room window`. The engine can continue local design work with these assumptions now; a later review revises the rules and reruns every dependent candidate.
